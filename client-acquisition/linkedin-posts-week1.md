## POST 1 — Monday
**Angle: Brand Storytelling (Afara Accelerator Case Study)**
**Target: Founders, fundraising-stage operators, accelerator cohort members**

---

A founder came to me with a solid business.

Real traction. A growing team. A product that worked.

But every investor conversation ended the same way — polite interest, no cheque.

We dug into why.

The business was ready. The story wasn't.

His deck opened with the market size. His bio read like a CV. His narrative started with what the company *does* — not why it *must exist*.

Investors weren't confused. They were unconvinced.

So we rebuilt everything from the ground up.

Not the product. The story.

We reframed the founding moment. We found the tension that made the problem feel urgent and personal. We built an investor narrative that answered the question they were actually asking: "Why this founder? Why now?"

The result?

Partnership offers from IFC and British International Investment.

The business didn't change. The story did.

Here's what I've learned after nearly two decades in storytelling across Nollywood, boardrooms, and pitch rooms:

Investors don't fund businesses. They fund conviction in a founder's story — one they can retell to their own stakeholders.

If your numbers are strong but your story isn't closing rooms, the story is the problem.

What does your investor narrative sound like right now?

## POST 2 — Wednesday
**Angle: Automation ("One Process" story)**
**Target: Founders and ops leads at 5-30 person companies, agency owners, e-commerce brands**

---

A client was spending 10 hours a week on one task.

Not a complicated task. Just a broken one.

Every time a new client signed, someone had to manually:
- Pull the contract from Google Drive
- Fill in the client details
- Send it for signature
- Chase for the signed copy
- Log it in their CRM
- Trigger the welcome email
- Update the project management board

Seven steps. Done by hand. Every. Single. Time.

They'd been doing it for two years. It was just "how things worked."

I spent 4 hours building the automation in n8n.

Now the whole sequence runs the moment a deal is marked closed in their CRM. Zero manual steps.

That's 10 hours a week back. Every week.

The insight that stays with me: **the processes that are costing you the most are the ones you've stopped noticing.**

You've normalised the inefficiency because it's always been there.

The businesses scaling fastest right now aren't just better at selling — they're better at *not wasting time on things machines can do.*

What's the most painful manual process in your business right now?

Drop it below. I read every reply.
